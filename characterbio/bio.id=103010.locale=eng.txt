<p>January 20th, 1942</p>

<p>Dear Mom, Dad and Mary, and Buck,</p>

<p>I hope that this letter finds you all well. I do miss you all. I dearly miss Louisville, the smell of the grass, the animals, and working at the track. I even miss the smell of horse dung! Funny the things you miss when you no longer have them around. There’s so much that I took for granted. Lord do I miss you...and I miss Buck! I can’t wait to scratch his ears again.</p>

<p>Right now, I’d give anything to sit on the front porch swing and listen to Dad play the guitar and drink a cold glass of lemonade. It was much hotter in the Philippines than Kentucky, and no fresh lemonade made it even more unbearable! What I wouldn’t give for that right now. I sure wish you could send some.</p>

<p>I figured that at last I have a bit of down time to write again, that I’d better take advantage of it since I’m sure you all have lots of questions. There are quite a lot of things that I can’t mention. In fact, as an officer it’s part of my job to read the enlisted men’s’ mail and censor sensitive items, what a drag that is.</p>

<p>The last time I wrote all of you it was about a month ago I guess, mid-December. I was still in the Philippines, and we’d just been attacked by those rotten Japanese! I wasn’t able to say much then of course, and with my injuries and all the commotion around here it was a bit hard to set my mind on letter writing. We were all having a heck of a time then. Let me tell you Mom and Dad, they hit us hard. We were overwhelmed and outmatched. They came out of nowhere. I managed to get into the air but our outdated, worn out airplanes (they don’t want us to say what kind) were just not up to the job. They walloped us, and we lost four pilots for our efforts. I barely even got into the air, and before I knew what was happening, I’d picked up a Zero on my tail and he had shot up wing!</p>

<p>I bailed out, still not sure how I did that, but somehow, I got out but I was much too low. I should be dead, but by the grace of God here I am. It was hell. Fire and screaming everywhere during the attack. I didn’t even notice my leg until later. Shrapnel? Or a bullet? I have no idea, but luckily whatever it was passed right through me. The bleeding was pretty bad though. When I looked down and saw my leg, I collapsed.</p>

<p>Well, you’ll be happy to know that I’m no longer in the Philippines. I was lucky to have been evacuated to Australia. Not everyone in the Philippines was as fortunate as me. I’m being sent back to the East Coast to finish my recuperation and be assigned to a new squadron. From there I’m not sure where I’ll end up. Europe... maybe the South Pacific… who knows.</p>

<p>My leg got pretty tore up, but Mom and Dad I promise that I’m on my way to full recovery and fighting shape! Whatever happens, or wherever we end up, we’ve been told that our days of flying those old tired airplanes are over and that we’ll have fresh, new aircraft underneath us next time.</p>

<p>I’ll let you know where in the States that I end up so you can send me some cookies. Your cookies are the best!</p>

<p>Give each other hugs from me, and tell Mary that she can have my room (for now). I know she always liked the window looking out over the road.</p>

<p>Give Buck lots and lots of hugs and ear scratches from me too!</p>

<p>I love you all.</p>

<p>As Ever,</p>
<p>$[firstName]</p>
