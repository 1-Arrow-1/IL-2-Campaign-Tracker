<p>“My boy’s no criminal, judge!" exclaimed an aggravated Roy $[lastName], standing up in the hot Alabama courtroom. He had been seated just behind his young son $[firstName] but could maintain his composure no longer. The judge did not bother to look at him, and said in a matter of fact tone, “Mr. $[lastName], please sit. Whether or not you think your boy is a criminal is immaterial. The fact of the matter is that constructing a still is illegal in the state of Alabama. In addition, your son has supplied stills to a number of local moonshiners, not to mention his own use of this equipment, AND distribution of its fruits, AND laws broke while distributing, including resisting arrest and every speed and traffic law on the books.”</p>

<p>He continued, looking down at the papers he was organizing on his pulpit.</p>

<p>“Now, from my perspective we have some mitigating circumstances, Mr. $[lastName] - the first of which is your boy’s unusually high marks in school. The second is the unusual quality and sophistication of the stills in question, which gave me an idea and has led to an interesting chain of events.” The judge finished shuffling the papers, then peered at the father of young $[firstName] over the top of his glasses.</p>

<p>“As it happens, Mr. $[lastName], I happen to know the governor personally and have filled him in as to the academic accomplishments of your boy, and also the ingenuity apparent in the “apparatus” constructed and sold by him. Because of his apparent intelligence, academic prowess, mechanical ingenuity, and shall we say “industriousness,” I have managed to acquire a letter of recommendation from the governor to Alabama State University for admission to this institution."</p>

<p>Looking up, he continued, “...where his smarts and talents may be developed and be put to use in a fashion that benefits our society rather than depletes it.”</p>

<p>The judge removed his glasses, and used them to vaguely point in Roy $[lastName]'s direction as he rested his elbow on the pulpit. “So you see, Mr. $[lastName], your boy’s abilities are going to land him in one of two places after he leaves this room: the state prison, or college. May I suggest, sir, that you choose the latter.”</p>

<p>So it was that a young $[name] found himself studying engineering at Alabama State University, a pursuit at which he found himself especially fitted. He did not escape trouble entirely, however, simply because he had found his way to an institution of learning. His adventures outrunning the local sheriff deputies around Monroe County had enabled him to develop driving skills that most professional race car drivers would envy. Thus, some impromptu (and some secretly scheduled) road races around the University neighborhood landed him in more legal hot water and nearly ended his college career. It was only one of his engineering professors strongly advocating for him and promising “permanent reformation” that narrowly saved his academic life.</p>

<p>When the Germans stormed through Poland and beyond, $[firstName] found the whole affair a mild curiosity but was not particularly moved or worried about the events, as he could not imagine the war affecting his life and career pursuits. A backwoods moonshiner who never planned to amount to anything, he slowly began to believe that he would be designing automobiles or perhaps aircraft one day.</p>

<p>When the Japanese attacked Pearl Harbor, however, $[firstName]’s sense of patriotism was awakened, and he along with most of his friends and classmates began to wonder whether their duties lay with finishing their education or with immediate service to their country. Eventually, the will to serve and to “put things right” had him visiting the local recruiting offices, and he considered his options of joining the Navy, the regular Army, and the Air Corps. In the end, his love of speed won out and he found himself walking back into the Air Corps recruiting office to meet his destiny in the sky.</p>

<p>During his extensive training, $[firstName] would find a few more near misses with authority, a few passes just a bit too low over the Keesler Field tarmac in Mississippi where he was sent to train, or rattling the shingles on a Mississippi farmhouse. In all this, he managed to cause just enough trouble to gain a reputation without going so far as to be ejected from the training program. In the end, he was rewarded with his $[startRank] wings, and with them securely fastened to his chest he would cross the Atlantic, far from the Alabama life that he had known and face his nation’s enemy in the skies over Europe.</p>
