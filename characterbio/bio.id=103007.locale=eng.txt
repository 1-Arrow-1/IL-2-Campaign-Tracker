<p>Born to the owner of a small general merchandise store in downtown Twin Falls, Idaho, $[name] spent most of his youth with his friends in the wilderness and running along the banks and cliffs of the Snake River. He also relished time in his father’s store, since his father had converted the rear of the store into a billiards or “pool room.” He loved to hang around and listen to the regulars tell stories from the Great War and other more mundane tales as they smoked and drank and casually played game after game on the green felt table. He would listen with rapt attention, often while nursing an orange soda that he had absconded with from the ice box at the front of the store.</p>

<p>While his mother strongly disliked her son spending so much time at the pool room, $[firstName]’s father would often remind her that “at least we know where he’s at, and he hasn’t drowned or fallen off a cliff,” which would end her protest immediately. His favorite tales were the fishing stories, with the fish somehow growing in size with each telling. His father ended up putting a sign over the pool table in the shape of a fish that read “Liars Central.” The name stuck and the pool room at the back of the store remained a young $[firstName]’s favorite spot throughout his childhood.</p>

<p>As he became older, $[firstName] was called upon more and more to man the front counter, stock the shelves, and generally help out with the business. While he enjoyed the social aspect of the job, he missed the carefree aspect of his younger days when his time was his own, with the biggest decision being whether to go fishing or hang out at Liars Central. Halfway through high school, with stories of trouble once again brewing in Germany, he began to ponder his path and wondered whether he’d be content to continue running the store as his father had done.</p>

<p>When $[firstName] was 16, a young mail delivery pilot named Sam Gunther began to frequent Liars Central, and his stories were a welcome change from the same stories of broken tractors or cars and the gallant, ingenious repairs they involved, general gossip, and big fish tales. The more he talked in the smoky room, the more young $[firstName] became intent on getting his own pilot’s license. When he asked Sam about learning to fly, Sam pulled the ever-present cigar from his mouth and set it in the tray on the side of the pool table. “Damn right, kid, you’re old enough, heck you only have to be 16 - meet me at the field tomorrow at 8 am, and I’ll introduce you to the instructor there.”</p>

<p>The lessons that followed were a financial strain on $[firstName]’s family; however, his parents and father especially (who had served in the Great War) believed that such skills would keep their son out of the trenches should the United States find itself once again at war. So, with their permission and support, young $[firstName] learned to fly. Logging as many hours as he could, he’d often go up with Jack, weaving his way along the Snake River canyon during mail runs or in between when time allowed.</p>

<p>When the U.S. entered the war, $[firstName] joined the Air Corps and was stationed at Moffett Field, California. Lacking the necessary education to be a pilot despite his experience, he worked as an enlisted man while he took college classes, his idea being that he would obtain a commission thereafter. Eventually he earned his degree and with over 300 hours of civilian flight time under his belt, his plan worked and he received his commission just after the attack on Pearl Harbor. He then entered basic flight training at Moffett Field and scored near the top of his class which saw him assigned to flying fighters.</p>

<p>“Mom and Dad, I’m leaving Camp Kilmer on the Queen Elizabeth tomorrow! Some buddies and I had crossed into New York to get a look at her, and you wouldn’t believe how big she is, she’s enormous, like a floating city just sitting there in the harbor. We’re all excited and a bit nervous as you might imagine, but we’re ready for whatever happens next. I guess a lot of G.I.s are crossing with us, but us officers will be spared the crowded bunks that the enlisted men have to endure. I feel a bit bad for them, but I’m not about to trade places with them, either!</p>

<p>I saw Sam Gunther during my training in California, he’d joined the Navy of all things, and was getting ready to ship out from San Francisco to somewhere in the Pacific. Never took him for a Navy guy, but from what I’ve seen in the Bay Area those boys do plenty of flying and are as good as anyone.</p>

<p>You’ll never guess who else I met, Jimmy Stewart! No kidding, he was finishing up training when I arrived at Moffett Field. I’m happy to report that he’s as nice as he seems in his movies, and acts just the same. He’s flying bombers I guess, although I’m not allowed to say what kind. Just goes to show that everyone is helping out, and I’m doing the right thing.</p>

<p>I miss you both, and the store, and Liars Central, and the peace of the river. Let everyone know that I’m doing fine and will write as often as I can, although I don’t know how often that will be once I’m over there, wherever “there” is.</p>

<p>I will see you again, love you both.</p>

<p>Sincerely,</p>
<p>$[firstName]”</p>
