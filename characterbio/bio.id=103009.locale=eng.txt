<p>Born on $[birthDate] to immigrant parents, the young $[name] grew up running in the streets of Brooklyn, New York while his parents and older brother ran a small butcher shop on the busy Front Street near the Brooklyn Bridge. While his parents would have preferred him to spend more time helping with the family business, his work ethic left something to be desired. When he did find himself at the butcher shop, his time there was usually short-lived, leading to him finding a more interesting, non-work related task to occupy his mind and satisfy his wanderlust.</p>

<p>Thus young $[firstName] was left to wander more and more with friends around the bustling waterfront. $[firstName] was a good athlete, but he found a fight on the streets as often as he found a stickball game. While rarely starting a confrontation, when it was time to throw a punch, he was the first to do so, a trait he carried into his young adult life. Because of this, he found himself waiting in the hallway outside of the school principal’s office, being dragged by the ear home or back to the butcher shop by the local police officer. Stickball games on the field at Flushing Airport resulted in similar troubles from authorities, but $[firstName] could not resist running under the aircraft as they landed or took off, even when a recent scolding and threat of more severe measures had temporarily stopped him and his friends from their airfield stickball endeavors.</p>

<p>It was not until $[firstName]’s 18th year that his parent’s work ethic began to take hold, and he found himself contributing to his family’s business on a regular basis. With the war brewing in Europe, there was less of a sense of playfulness among him and his peers around the borough. Daily reports on the radio recounted battles won and lost, of lives taken, and scores of families displaced, or worse. The sense of pessimism from the President himself in his frequent addresses was palpable.</p>

<p>$[firstName]’s slightly older friend Monty Salinger, who had enlisted in the Army Reserves and had been stationed at the airfield, encouraged him meet an Army Air Corps recruiter who was touring the boroughs and would be at the airport for a few days. “You can't be cutting up dead cows your whole life, ya know?” was all that Monty offered as a rationale for coaxing $[firstName] into meeting the recruiter. Not convinced of his friend’s logic, he finally agreed to meet the recruiter if only to stop the endless badgering on the matter.</p>

<p>$[firstName] talked with the sharply dressed recruiter for over an hour, and despite not having given the possibility a serious thought before this day, $[firstName] was convinced that the Air Corps was how he would serve his country and face down the Germans and Japanese. He became a mechanic and over some months transferred between bases at Tulsa and St. Louis before one day seeing a flyer posted on the bulletin board for “Flying Sergeant” that promised to turn him into a pilot. He enrolled, as did Monty and soon it seemed they were both on their way to becoming aviators for the country that they both loved and wanted so much to serve, especially should the war find its way to their shores.</p>

<p>On his first flight in the backseat of a trainer, $[firstName] became sick and was certain that he had chosen the wrong path and set his mind to remaining with his feet planted on the earth thereafter. The Lieutenant running the training convinced him to give it another try, and after a few more attempts in the trainer his stomach and his will to become a pilot both aligned and he had no further troubles. From that point on he did well in his flight training, and while some of the class work caused him some difficulties at times, his road to earning his wings went largely without a problem until one day near the end of his training his friend Monty was killed near their training base at Randolph Field, Texas when he failed to pull out of a dive.</p>

<p>Monty was not the first victim of a training accident nor the last friend he would lose during the war, but loss was a fact of life by now for $[firstName]. When such an event happened, you simply had to move on. Laying in his bunk at Camp Kilmer, New Jersey the night before leaving for England, he said to himself - “OK then, let’s get on with it.”</p>
