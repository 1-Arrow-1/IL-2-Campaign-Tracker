<p>“If it’s worth doing, it’s worth doing perfectly” was the oft-repeated mantra that young $[name] grew up hearing from his grandfather while growing up in Yakima, Washington. This was a small town on the eastern side of the Cascade Mountains named after the Indian tribe that called the area home, known for its rich soil as well as its peach and cherry orchards. A used car salesman, $[firstName]’s grandfather became known as “honest John” around the small semi-arid town in Eastern Washington. His integrity and honesty at his job and his unwavering presence in the local church earned him a large number of loyal friends, and it was a rare evening when their front porch was not entertaining one of these many people who had come to know his grandfather.</p>

<p>In the evenings after his homework was completed, $[firstName] would often sit at the kitchen table and with the help of his father and grandfather build model aircraft until it was time for him to finally go to bed. When a model was finished, they would decide together where it would hang in his room and whether it was winning or losing the fight. German aircraft of course were always the losers in these static dogfights that became swirling melees in his imagination. Like so many boys, his interest in aviation was also strongly fueled by the stories in comic books and short fiction novels that his father would bring him from the local gas station.</p>

<p>$[firstName]’s grandfather never missed a day of church, and it was this powerfully instilled sense of integrity as well as strong faith that pervaded his character in his formative years, qualities that he carried with him into his young manhood. He was a bit of an introvert, never one to make himself the center of attention, but always willing to offer help to anyone who needed it, just as his grandfather and father did. Never one to choose a fight at school, he was also never one to allow another to be bullied, and would on several occasions place himself between someone and a more powerful opponent intent on doing harm.</p>

<p>As a member of his high school baseball team, $[firstName] excelled in his first year, and by his senior year, he was entertaining hopes of playing in college if he could save enough money. As his high school career was waning and the sun had its grip on the Yakima valley, so too did Hitler begin to tighten his grip on Europe. He was growing into a respected member of his small rural hometown, taking classes at the community college and helping his grandfather run the car dealership. All the while he pondered his college and baseball prospects when one December morning in 1941, the Japanese bombed the American naval base at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii.</p>

<p>$[firstName]’s feelings were echoed by most young men across the country in the days following this attack: patriotism and anger mixed with determination fueled en masse a generation of young men in the winter of 1941, and by the scores they would descend to recruiting stations across the country. He immediately knew that he wanted to become a pilot, and after learning that he needed two years of college, he joined the Washington Air National Guard at Felts Field near Spokane while he finished his final semester.</p>

<p>$[firstName] then received a transfer into the Army Air Corps and began his training at Moffet Field, California. His lessons from his grandfather would follow him always and serve him throughout his training, and in the difficult and trying months and years to follow, high in the skies over Europe.</p>
