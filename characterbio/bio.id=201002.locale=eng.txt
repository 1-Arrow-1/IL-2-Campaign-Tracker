<p>Coming from a nation obsessed with physical fitness, $[name] still stood out as a dedicated sportsman. As a schoolboy, he was considered a slow but persistent learner; this persistence was no doubt honed over long hours of running, jumping, and skiing rather than doing his homework. His interest in aviation was typical for boys his age - typical for the generation of children raised on the war stories of Richthofen, Boelcke, and other Great War aviation heroes. After he finished his secondary schooling with no money for his dream of entering civilian aviation school, young $[firstName] planned to become a physical education teacher and maybe seek fame as decathalon athlete. Around the same time, the Luftwaffe started recruiting volunteers for pilot training and, to his surprise, $[firstName] passed the rigorous entrance exams without much difficulty.</p>
<p>His first combat assignment started a trend that would dominate - and almost destroy - his piloting career. Ever slow to learn new techniques and familiarize with new planes, he was seen as a poor pilot by his superiors. Other pilots saw him as an odd killjoy, refusing to drink and smoke and preferring running and soccer to socializing in the mess.</p>
<p>When war broke out in 1939, $[firstName] was denied a combat flying assignment. He was always the first to be posted away when his Kommodore had to give up a pilot, only to persistently apply for transfer to another combat unit. He spent the invasion of Poland behind a desk, the invasion of France grounded, the Battle of Britain and the invasion of Yugoslavia in a training reserve unit, and the invasion of Greece hauling damaged planes to a repair park before eventually flying them back. He practiced gunnery, bombing, and formation flying as rigorously as he pursued playing sports, but by the time he became a competent pilot his reputation had already preceded him, and so his Kommodores tried to get rid of him as soon as he arrived.</p>

His reputation preceded him once again but, rather than get rid of a bad pilot, his new Kommodore posted $[firstName] to a unit whose Kommandeur he disliked as calculated insult. After 2 years of dead-end posts, at age of $[age], $[startRank] $[name] prepared to take off for his first combat sortie. It was a new beginning he worked long and hard for, and he trusted that his persistence would see him through.</p>
