<p>Born in a rural part of Bavaria in $[birthDate] as the younger of two brothers, $[name] was an aviator from his childhood. Like many German schoolboys of his generation he was part of the local flying club, using a primitive glider that he and his friends built themselves in a shed. After completing his secondary schooling, $[firstName] took a government-sponsored piloting course while studying aviation engineering at the University of Wurzburg. However, he never finished the studies, instead accepting a job offer in the newly formed <i>Lufthansa</i> as soon as he became a certified pilot. At the beginning of his civilian career, he primarily flew Junkers Ju 52s as a copilot across continental Europe. However, $[firstName] was not content with this - he wanted to be in command of his own plane and to fly even larger planes on longer routes. His drive to excel was eventually rewarded with a promotion to Flugkapitan and an assignment to Focke-Wulf Fw 200s on long-distance routes to southeast Asia.</p>
<p>After the Luftwaffe was created in 1935, the Reich put out a call for pilots, whose training the government would pay for. Subsequently, in 1937, $[lastName] was compelled to join the Luftwaffe as a civilian instructor. He adamantly refused enlistment into the Luftwaffe until late 1938, when one too many of his cadets - an infantry Hauptmann trying to become a pilot – refused to take orders from a mere civilian. $[firstName] enlisted and the next day the Hauptmann was furious to be fired from the course by the instructor he outranked. $[lastName] spent the next couple of years rising through the ranks as a valued instructor. A flier by calling and soldier by necessity, he was preparing pilots that took part in the invasions of Poland, Norway, the Low Countries, and France, and fought in the Battle of Britain. He was content to fly, to teach and to stay out of the fighting until a personal tragedy changed everything.</p>
<p>August, the older brother of $[firstName], had volunteered to join the Luftwaffe to avoid being drafted into the Heer. He served as a pilot of a Ju 88 bomber; in October 1940 his plane crashed during takeoff for a night bombing sortie over England, killing the entire crew. Blaming himself for staying in piloting school while his brother fought and died, $[firstName] set out to become a combat pilot himself. Despite the efforts of his superiors to keep him as an instructor, $[startRank] $[lastName] passed through additional training and, on $[startDate], began his service with $[startSquadronName]. His days of peaceful flying were over.</p>
