<p>Baron $[name] was born in $[birthDate] in Wetteren, Belgium. His father was an artist, and so it was from him he learned to observe the world around him with a keen eye for detail.</p>
<p>$[firstName] studied science at the University of Leuven. His experiments with hydrogen and electricity turned out to be a dangerous combination; after an explosion one spring semester he was expelled and had to return home for the rest of the year.</p> 
<p>Like most Belgian men he would have to complete some military service, so he was conscripted into the army and served with the Premiere Regiment Grenadiers. After his service ended he decided to stay in the military and signed up for a longer enlistment.</p>
<p>In 1914, following the German invasion of Belgium, Baron $[name] transferred to the Motor Machine Gun Corps. They swept through Flanders on their motorcycles, carrying Vickers machine guns. Once the fighting on the Western Front had settled down into the stalemate of trench warfare, the opportunities for his unit to operate as a mobile force became limited. Eager to serve his country, Baron $[name] began looking around for other things to do...</p> 
<p>On 10 September 1915, he signed up for flight training in the Compagnie des Aviateurs, but his request was rejected due to inadequacies in Belgian training. Baron $[name] subsequently took eight weeks leave to train at his own expense (along with 39 other Belgians) at a civilian flying school in Hendon, England. There he received lessons from, amongst others, Albert Ball. Two months later this was followed by further training in France at the Farman military aviation school in etampes.</p>
<p>On December 12th, 1915 Baron $[name] received his pilot's license. He joined the 6e Escadrille at Houthem in Belgium, where he flew several reconnaissance flights. He spent most of his free time in his hangar, working on his plane and learning from the mechanics.</p>
<p>Baron $[name] was commended for his bravery during a daring escape from three enemy planes who chased him relentlessly over the front lines. After he landed, he was amazed by the heavy bullet damage the plane had sustained. Soon afterwards, he asked to be transferred to a unit flying aircraft that were more capable than his slow-flying recon plane. His superiors recognised that he was an excellent pilot, and so he was re-assigned to $[startSquadronName] on $[startDate].</p>
