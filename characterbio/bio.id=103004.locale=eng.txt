<p>Born on $[birthDate] to parents Wyatt and Millie $[lastName], $[firstName] grew up on a 4,000-acre cattle ranch in Montana that had been in his family since shortly after the Civil War. The oldest of five children, he was the quintessential western cattle rancher and genuine cowboy born of the old breed that settled the land early in the 19th century. His father was, in a word, “rugged,” tough, quiet, with a brand of stoicism that can only be called “cowboy.” His temperament was steady as an oak until he had a good reason to behave otherwise; he was a man slow to anger. Loyal to his family and with a strong faith in God and an unshakable work ethic, $[firstName]’s father seemed to come right out of a pulp magazine cowboy story.</p>

<p>It was these qualities that imbued the young $[firstName] as he grew into his young adulthood. His mother would often say “still waters run deep” when describing her son, and it was a phrase that fit him and his father well. He would often take long rides on the ranch under the Montana sky, monitoring the herd, or hunting coyotes with his Winchester Model 54 rifle that his father had gifted him on his 15th birthday. He would ride for hours alone, or sometimes with his father or uncle, ignoring the weather, be it rain beating down and dripping from his worn Stetson cowboy hat, or the winter snows or heat of the summer. He was unshakable once his mind was set to a task, yet another quality inherited from his elders.</p>

<p>“A good man in a storm” was probably the most meaningful compliment that $[firstName] had received in his 19th year, especially since it came from his father, the man whom he admired and respected above all others. Hardly the type one would expect to attend college, from roughly the age of 12 both his father and mother had expressed their opinions that their young son had the smarts and determination to get a good education. Thus, his mother and father both were strong driving forces in his teenage life, making sure that he kept up with his studies.</p>

<p>$[firstName] was prone to disappearing for most of the day on his horse “Domingo” before finally returning to complete a school assignment due the following day. “Nothing wrong with being a smart cowboy” his mother would often call out from the kitchen when she would finally hear the screen door swing shut as he returned home after such an episode. His resistance to school, however, gradually faded, and he became a dedicated student for the final few years of high school.</p>

<p>After high school, $[firstName] applied and was accepted to the Montana State University Agricultural and Range Sciences program. After his first 18 months he even toyed with the idea of veterinary school, and felt he was possibly at a crossroads in his schooling career when on December 7, 1941 the Japanese attacked Pearl Harbor. Unlike some of his friends and schoolmates, he did not make a trip to the recruiting office immediately, instead choosing to continue his studies for as long as possible. It was not until his classmate, a young man named Mathew Bell enrolled in the Army Air Corps, then proceeded with attempts to convince $[firstName] to do likewise that his plans began to waiver.</p>

<p>Mathew, who had obtained his pilot’s license through the Civilian Pilot Training Program, had been using a Piper Cub on his father’s ranch to help monitor the herd and hunt coyotes. “Come on, $[firstName], you’d be great at it, and when you’re done you can get your own plane and use it on the ranch like we do. Tell me you've never heard a better idea!” He continued, “Besides, we’re all going to end up fighting eventually, better to choose your destiny now.”</p>

<p>Young $[firstName] found that he had a hard time arguing with his friend’s logic, and he liked the idea of learning to fly. He often gazed up at and admired the young pilot who delivered mail in and around Phillips County in his brightly painted Hawker biplane that he had imported from Britain. The two shared a bit of a weekly tradition of waving at each other as the pilot flew low over a particular knoll on the range that $[firstName] would be waiting at at the same time each week.</p>

<p>So it was that $[firstName] found himself at the Army Air Corps recruiting office some days later, setting himself on a course that would forever change his life and sending him hurtling into the awaiting maelstrom started by a madman an ocean away.</p>
