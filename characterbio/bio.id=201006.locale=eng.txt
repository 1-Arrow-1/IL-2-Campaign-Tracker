<p>Born on $[birthDate] in Upper Silesia, then part of the German Empire, $[name] was a son of the town dentist. As a young man he participated in the Great War, first as an infantryman and an NCO. After his first wound and recovery, he became a pilot - first in recon two-seaters before moving on to fighters in Jasta 32. The second wound caused by his would-be first victory put him out of the fight- by the time he recovered, the war was over, and the Empire was lost. $[lastName] fought to preserve the German state in the <i>Freikorpsen</i>, against communist and pro-Polish insurgents but found it a hollow and disgusting enterprise. In his eyes, civil war was more dirty and brutal than being in the trenches, His own comrades were almost as harmful to Germany as the insurgents they fought against, while his home town was lost to the new Polish state in a plebiscite.</p>
<p>Like many Great War veterans, $[firstName] tried his strength as a businessman, only to repeatedly fail. Fortunately, many of his wartime friends made careers in the Reichswehr, and so by 1930, $[lastName] was a commissioned officer. With creation of the Luftwaffe he briefly became an instructor, before being promoted to a command position in one of the new Kampfgeschwaderen. He commanded from behind a desk for the first two years of the war, sending bomber pilots on missions in all of the European campaigns but gradually realizing that the war he was fighting for was little better than the street fighting of the 1920s.</p>
<p>$[firstName] reached his limit during the early invasion of the Soviet Union, when was visited by two low ranking SS-men. They requested as many men as $[lastName] could spare to form up squads for a "special assignment." $[lastName] ordered his entire command to assemble in full dress uniform, explained the request of the SS and threatened to resign if any of his men volunteered. To their honor, none did.</p>
<p>The German High Command was furious with $[lastName], and his former comrade Herman Goring was not willing to protect him. Instead, he was stripped of his command and given an offer he could not refuse - to "volunteer" for active service as a pilot. And so, on $[startDate], freshly-demoted $[startRank] $[name] began to fly with $[startSquadronName]. He accepted the change gladly, giving up responsibilities he no longer wanted and ties to a regime he had given up on. The Nazis are no doubt hoping the enemy will kill him; he's not a young man anymore, the planes are faster, gunnery more difficult and pace of air combat much more different than anything he previously experienced. $[firstName] does not mind; he can fight the enemies of the German people - the only cause still worth fighting for - without second thoughts. Perhaps this is his second chance.</p>
